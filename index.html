THE FEATURE

From working the floor to owning the room

For Binod Gautam, entrepreneurship began with a simple conclusion: years of experience could become the foundation for something independent.

Before opening Gym Junkies, Gautam worked in several gyms in Bhaktapur. The work built practical knowledge, but he felt employment alone was not improving his financial position enough. Starting his own venture offered a path toward independence—and a chance to shape the service around his own priorities.

That path was not small or inexpensive. A gym demands space, machines and ongoing upkeep from day one. Because the initial investment was more than he could manage alone, Gautam brought in a partner. Once the doors opened, machine servicing, maintenance, refreshments and other everyday costs became part of the operating equation.

THE BUSINESS MODEL

Accessible by price.
Personal by design.

01

Affordability

At around NPR 2,000 per month, the gym aims to remain reachable for local customers even as competition increases.

02

Individual attention

Gautam says every trainee arrives with a different body, goal or concern. The service starts by understanding the individual.

03

Quality under pressure

Customers look for lower prices, but price competition cannot be allowed to erode equipment, guidance or service quality.

THE MARKET

Fitness is becoming part of everyday life

Gautam sees growing demand as modern food habits and sedentary routines push more people to think seriously about fitness. His observation is visible in the gym’s customer mix: young people who have completed SEE train alongside adults in their sixties.

This reflects the interviewee’s experience and viewpoint; it is not medical advice.
